#### Canadian General Social Survey on Giving, Volunteering, and Participating, 2018 (cycle 33)
| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Sample type** | The sample is based on a stratified design employing probability sampling. The stratification is done at the province/census metropolitan area (CMA) level. Information is collected from one randomly selected household member aged 15 or older, and proxy responses are not permitted. |
| **Sample size** | A field sample of approximately 50,000 units was used. Among them, about 40,000 invitation letters to the electronic questionnaire were sent to selected households across Canada. A completion of 24,000 questionnaires was expected. |
| **Target population** | The target population for the GSS Giving, Volunteering, and Participating includes all persons 15 years of age and older living in the ten provinces of Canada. It excludes full-time (residing for more than six months) residents of institutions. |
| **Sampling frame** | This survey uses a frame that combines landline and cellular telephone numbers from the Census and various administrative sources with Statistics Canada's dwelling frame. Records on the frame are groups of one or several telephone numbers associated with the same address (or a single telephone number in the case a link between a telephone number and an address could not be established). This sampling frame is used to obtain better coverage of households with a telephone number. |
| **Survey mode(s)** | Data are collected directly from survey respondents either through an electronic questionnaire or through CATI (computer-assisted telephone interviewing). No proxy reporting is allowed. The respondents had the choice between French and English. The average time to complete the survey is estimated at 44 minutes. |
| **Timeline** | Every 5 years, from September to December. The data for the 2018 survey was collected from September 4, 2018, to December 28, 2018. |
| **Response rate** | The overall response rate is 41.9%. |
| **Weights** | Weighting adjustments were made to ensure the sample accurately represents the population demographics. A weighting factor (WGHT_PER) is available on the microdata file for analysis at the person level. Additionally, bootstrap weights were created for design-based variance estimation. The weights were also adjusted to match the 2017 CIS distribution by province. |
| **Data processing** | Processing used the SSPE set of generalized processing steps and utilities to allow subject matter and survey support staff to specify and run the processing of the survey in a timely fashion with high-quality outputs. Edits were performed automatically and manually at various stages of processing at macro and micro levels. All survey records were subjected to computer edits throughout the course of the interview. If the interviewer was unable to correctly resolve the detected errors, the interviewer bypassed the edit and forwarded the data to head office for resolution. |
| **Cleaning, imputation, etc.** | Imputation was carried out in nine steps. Donor imputation and mean imputation among a pool of donors were used to fill in missing data. Income information was obtained by linking to the tax data of respondents who had not objected to the linkage. The GVP imputation process worked well and helped to fill incomplete responses with the experience of other respondents with similar or identical characteristics. |
| **Sources of error** | Sampling error, non-sampling error, coverage error, response error, processing error. Non-sampling errors were minimized by using a well-tested questionnaire, a proven methodology, specialized interviewers, and strict quality control. |
| **Limitations, known biases** | Coverage errors due to households without telephones or with telephone services not covered by the frame. Non-response bias reduced by adjusting survey weights. Other non-sampling errors minimized through rigorous quality assurance mechanisms and validation measures. |
| **Citation** | Statistics Canada. (2021). Canadian General Social Survey on Giving, Volunteering, and Participating, 2018 (Cycle 33) [Data set]. |
| **Links to any documentation or additional sources used** | [General Social Survey on Giving, Volunteering, and Participating, 2018 (Cycle 33) Documentation](https://www23.statcan.gc.ca/imdb/p2SV.pl?Function=getSurvey&Id=796234) |

#### Canadian Election Study Online Survey, 2019, conducted by Laura Stephenson, Allison Harell, Daniel Rubenson and Peter Loewen
| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Sample type** | The online sample was composed of a two-wave panel with a modified rolling-cross section during the campaign period and a post-election recontact wave. Sampling was stratified by region and balanced on gender and age within each region.|
| **Sample size** | Pre-election survey: 37,822 respondents. Post-election survey: 10,340 respondents.|
| **Target population**| Canadian citizens and permanent residents aged 18 or older.|
| **Sampling frame**| Online panels procured through Qualtrics, with targets stratified by region, gender, and age. Specific regional quotas and language preferences within regions were aimed for during sampling.|
| **Survey mode(s)** | Online survey. The survey instrument was presented on the Qualtrics online platform.|
| **Timeline**| Campaign Period Survey: September 13th to October 21st, 2019. Post-election survey: October 24th to November 11th, 2019.|
| **Response rate**| Not explicitly stated, but the number of interviews completed provides insight into participation rates.|
| **Weights**| Weight variables are provided to ensure that the data is representative of the population. Weights were created using an iterative "raking" process according to province, gender, age group, and education level. Four different weight variables are available depending on the sample portion used.|
| **Data processing**| Survey sampling, programming, and weighting were performed under the supervision of the study authors. The data includes identification of panel membership and quality checks. Data cleaning involved removing incomplete responses, duplicates, speeders, straightliners, and those with postal code mismatches.|
| **Cleaning, imputation, etc.** | Duplicate responses and low-quality data (e.g., speeders, straightliners) were removed. Specific flags were used to identify inattentive respondents and initial duplicates. Post-election survey responses were matched to campaign period responses using panel IDs and limited demographic information.|
| **Sources of error**| Potential errors include sampling error, non-sampling error, coverage error, and non-response bias. Efforts to minimize these errors included using a well-tested questionnaire, iterative weighting, and rigorous data cleaning processes.|
| **Limitations, known biases** | Coverage limitations include excluding respondents from the territories and those not covered by the online panels. Non-response bias was adjusted for using survey weights. Known biases related to online survey mode and panel recruitment strategies.|
| **Citation** | Stephenson, Laura B., Allison Harell, Daniel Rubenson, and Peter John Loewen. The 2019 Canadian Election Study – Online Collection. [dataset]|
| **Links to any documentation or additional sources used** | [Canadian Election Study Online Survey (2019) Documentation](https://dataverse.harvard.edu/dataset.xhtml?persistentId=doi:10.7910/DVN/DUS88V)|

#### Trophic niche flexibility in Glossophaga soricina: how nectar seeker sneaks an insect snack, conducted by Elizabeth Clare et al.
| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Sample type** | Targeted sampling of Glossophaga soricina bats in their natural habitat.|
| **Sample size** | 112 bats caught (73 females, 39 males); 38 fecal samples obtained.|
| **Target population** | Glossophaga soricina (nectar-feeding bats).|
| **Sampling frame** | Specific geographical locations in Costa Rica where the bats were found.|
| **Survey mode(s)** | Field observations, laboratory experiments, and DNA barcoding.|
| **Timeline** | Not specified, but involves field surveys and laboratory work over a period of time.|
| **Response rate** | Not applicable.|
| **Weights** | Not applicable.|
| **Data processing** | Analysis included DNA barcoding of fecal samples, acoustic analysis of echolocation calls, and behavioral observations. |
| **Cleaning, imputation, etc.** | Removal of duplicate or low-quality data points was not applicable. Emphasis was on accurate recording and analysis of bat behavior and diet. |
| **Sources of error** | Potential errors include sampling bias, identification errors in DNA barcoding, and observer bias in behavioral experiments. |
| **Limitations, known biases** | Limited to specific geographical areas and sample sizes. Behavioral observations may be influenced by the laboratory setting. Identification of insect prey was not always conclusive due to incomplete reference databases.|
| **Citation** | Clare, E., et al. (2014). Trophic niche flexibility in Glossophaga soricina: how nectar seeker sneaks an insect snack. Functional Ecology, 28, 632–641. https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2435.12192 |
| **Links to any documentation or additional sources used** | [Functional Ecology 2014, 28, 632–641](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2435.12192)|
